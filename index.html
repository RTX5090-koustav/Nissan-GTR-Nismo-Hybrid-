H₂ TANK→V8 + GENERATOR→AWDBATTERY⇄E-MOTOR⇄
01 / LAUNCH

Torque, immediately.

The battery powers an integrated motor through the transfer case to both axles. The V8 starts and synchronises as you approach the selected handover RPM.

02 / HANDOVER

Eight cylinders take over.

A 90° V8 burns hydrogen from the rear tank. A short torque blend connects the engine through the transmission to all four wheels.

03 / RECOVERY

Energy comes back.

Braking turns the motor into a generator. Engine charging uses extra hydrogen. Recovery has losses, and a full battery cannot accept more charge.

Prototype assumptions & scientific notes

This is a hydrogen internal-combustion hybrid, replacing the original V-shaped fuel-cell teaching model. It does not use fuel-cell membranes or proton flow. Hydrogen combustion can produce nitrogen oxides; this is not a zero-emissions claim.

The simplified GT-R concept assumes a 2,500 kg vehicle, 12 kWh battery, 6 kg tank, 140 kW electric drive, 300 kW engine, 18 kW engine charging and up to 70 kW regeneration. These are editable design assumptions, not Range Rover specifications or tested outputs. The model uses one effective drive ratio for clarity, with no gear shifting. Piston motion is slowed down for inspection.

Battery charging stops at 80% from the engine and 100% from regeneration. Low battery overrides electric launch if hydrogen remains. With neither energy source available, only coasting and friction braking remain. The user-supplied GT-R exterior model is used for the car body. Hybrid component packaging and system behaviour are illustrative, not manufacturer CAD or tested conversion specifications.
